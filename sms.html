Compliance

We don't do cold texts. And neither should you.

Texting a homeowner who never asked isn't just rude — under the TCPA it can cost you four figures per message. Consent-based SMS isn't the cautious version of the channel. It's the version that works: the text lands mid-conversation, so it reads like service, not spam.

Express consent, recorded

A sequence can only start from a reply or an opt-in. The consent — its time, its source thread — is stored on the home's record, so you can prove it later.

STOP means stop

Opt-outs process instantly and suppress the number across every sequence, journey, and manual send. There is no re-add button.

Quiet hours by local time

Sends hold outside 8am–9pm in the homeowner's time zone — the TCPA window — and queue politely for morning. No 6am buzz, ever.

A registered sender

10DLC registration in your business name means carriers treat your traffic as legitimate business messaging — delivered to the handset, not the filter.

Is this compliant?

It's built to be. Sequences can only start from a reply or an opt-in, and the consent is recorded — time and source thread — on the home's record. STOP is honored instantly, quiet hours are enforced in the homeowner's local time, and your number is 10DLC-registered to your business. There is no feature in the product that can send a cold text.

Can I text cold lists?

No — and you don't want to. Cold SMS risks TCPA penalties that run four figures per message, and it converts terribly anyway, because a text from a stranger reads as spam. If you want to open conversations with homeowners who haven't heard from you, that's what managed email and postcards are for. SMS is where the conversation continues.

Do texts come from my number?

From a dedicated local number registered to your business — an area code local to your market, 10DLC-registered in your name, and yours alone for as long as you're on the platform. Homeowners can call or text it back, and you can pick up any thread by hand from the two-way inbox.
